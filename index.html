Core music theory essentials

The Staff: Music is written on five lines and four spaces.

Treble Clef Lines: E, G, B, D, F (Every Good Boy Does Fine)

Treble Clef Spaces: F, A, C, E (FACE)

Bass Clef Lines: G, B, D, F, A (Good Boys Do Fine Always)

Major formula: Whole - Whole - Half - Whole - Whole - Whole - Half

Natural minor formula: Whole - Half - Whole - Whole - Half - Whole - Whole

Example: C major: C D E F G A B C

Example: A minor: A B C D E F G A

Interval: The distance between two notes.

Unison: Same pitch

Second: One step apart

Third: Common in chords and melodies

Perfect fifth: Bright, stable sound

Key signature: Tells you which notes are sharp or flat in a piece.

Sharp (#): Raises a note by a half step.

Flat (b): Lowers a note by a half step.

Natural (♮): Cancels a sharp or flat.

Top number: Beats per measure.

Bottom number: Which note value gets one beat.

4/4 time: Four quarter-note beats per measure.

3/4 time: Common in waltzes and dances.

Chord: Three or more notes played together.

Triad: Root, third, and fifth.

C major triad: C - E - G

Major vs minor: Major sounds bright, minor sounds darker or sadder.
